1. Know your Tokyo airport & terminal

Each Tokyo airport uses separate rail stations and pickup layouts — confirm whether your ticket says HND or NRT and which terminal handles your flight.

Haneda (HND)

Terminal 3 (& Intl T2)

Most international airlines land at HND Terminal 3 (select ANA international flights arrive at Terminal 2). Both the Keikyu Line and Tokyo Monorail stop directly inside.

Narita (NRT)

Terminals 1 & 2 Rail

Major full-service international terminals with basement rail stations served directly by both the Keisei Skyliner and JR Narita Express (N’EX).

Narita T3 · No Rail

Terminal 3 (Low-Cost)

Serving regional low-cost carriers with no train platform at T3 — walk ~10–15 minutes via the covered walkway or take the free shuttle bus to Terminal 2 for rail and buses.

2. Getting from Haneda or Narita to central Tokyo

Tokyo’s airport rail links are clean, fast and punctual during the day, but neither airport runs all-night trains — late arrivals after ~22:00 at Narita or midnight at Haneda require overnight buses, taxis or a pre-booked transfer.

Tips for Tokyo trains & IC cards

Keikyu, Tokyo Monorail, subway lines and standard JR trains support digital Suica/PASMO in Apple Wallet or physical Welcome Suica / PASMO Passport cards — though reserved express tickets (Keisei Skyliner and JR Narita Express) require buying an express seat ticket in addition to the base fare. If carrying multiple oversized suitcases onto regular commuter trains, consider luggage forwarding (takkyubin) counters in the arrivals lobby.

Tips for late-night arrivals

Allow 45–75 minutes after scheduled touchdown for Japanese immigration, Visit Japan Web QR checks and baggage claim. A flight arriving at Narita at 20:55 may easily miss the ~21:44 Narita Express; confirm late bus schedules or arrange pickup in advance rather than risking stranded transfers.

4. Quick Tokyo arrival checklist

  1. Confirm HND vs. NRT and your landing hour. Check last-train cutoffs (especially N’EX ~21:44 and Skyliner ~22:30 from Narita).
  2. Screenshot your Visit Japan Web QR codes & hotel address. Save offline copies before takeoff alongside your hotel’s nearest station exit.
  3. Prepare Japan mobile data before boarding. Install your Japan eSIM at home or plan Wi-Fi pickup counters if you need multiple laptops connected.
  4. Sort out Suica/PASMO early. Add digital Suica/PASMO to Apple Wallet before landing (Android visitors can pick up physical cards at airport rail counters).

Common questions

Is Haneda (HND) always closer to central Tokyo than Narita (NRT)?

Yes. Haneda is about 15–30 minutes by Keikyu or Monorail from central hubs like Shinagawa and Hamamatsucho. Narita is around 60 km east of central Tokyo, taking ~36–60+ minutes by express rail depending on where your hotel is located.

What happens if my flight arrives at Narita after 22:00?

The JR Narita Express typically finishes around 21:44–21:47 and the Keisei Skyliner around 22:30. After those times, you will rely on late-night Airport Limousine Buses (where running), expensive long-distance metered taxis, or a pre-booked private transfer.

Does Narita Terminal 3 have a train station?

No — Narita Terminal 3 is linked to Terminal 2 by a ~10–15 minute covered walkway and free terminal shuttle bus. Catch both the Keisei Skyliner and JR Narita Express from Narita Terminal 2 station.

Should I get a Japan eSIM or rent a pocket Wi-Fi at the airport?

For solo travellers or couples with unlocked eSIM-compatible phones, a travel eSIM avoids airport counter queues, carrying/charging a second battery pack and returning hardware before departure. Pocket Wi-Fi still makes sense if several travellers share Wi-Fi across non-eSIM devices.
