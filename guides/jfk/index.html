1. Know your JFK arrival terminal

JFK’s multi-billion-dollar redevelopment means roadway detours, realigned pickup zones and phased gate moves around the central loop. Confirm your exact terminal before stepping out to Arrivals.

Terminals 1 & 4

Major International Hubs

Terminal 4 handles Delta and many long-haul international carriers (allow time for long concourse walks and customs). Terminal 1 handles several European and Asian airlines.

Terminal 8

British Airways, AA & Oneworld

Primary hub for American Airlines, British Airways, Iberia and partner carriers. Direct indoor bridge access to the Terminal 8 AirTrain station and outside taxi dispatch.

Terminals 5, 7 & New T6

JetBlue, Realigned T7 & Phased T6

Terminal 5 serves JetBlue (watch signage for construction pickup detours). Terminal 7 routes rides through the T7 garage frontage. New Terminal 6 is opening in phases — verify gate announcements on arrival day.

2. Getting from JFK to Manhattan or Brooklyn

Manhattan road traffic can swing a 40-minute drive into 90+ minutes during weekday rush hours. Rail via AirTrain JFK gives predictable timing for solo travellers and light bags, while taxis and pre-booked transfers save stair climbs when travelling as a group.

Tips for AirTrain JFK & LIRR

Riding the AirTrain between terminals (for example, changing from Terminal 4 to Terminal 8) is free. You only pay the $8.75 AirTrain fare at the fare gates when exiting or entering at Jamaica or Howard Beach stations — use any contactless Visa/Mastercard/Amex or phone wallet (OMNY), or check official AirTrain JFK information for current routes.

Tips for official NYC taxis

Follow signs inside your terminal to the official uniformed taxi dispatcher — never accept rides from people approaching you in the Arrivals hall. For Manhattan destinations, check the meter shows Rate #02 (Manhattan–JFK) and keep small room in your budget for Midtown Tunnel tolls and surcharges listed on NYC TLC’s official taxi fare guide.

3. US mobile data & eSIM on arrival

JFK offers free terminal Wi-Fi inside arrivals, which is helpful while queuing at US immigration or checking your AirTrain stop. Once you step outside to meet a driver, check MTA subway directions or order a ride in Queens, standard UK and EU home plans often charge daily US roaming fees or throttle data speeds.

Check your existing mobile plan’s United States roaming rate first. If US roaming is excluded or capped, installing a travel eSIM before you depart from home lets you switch on local 4G/5G data as soon as the aircraft reaches the gate.

4. Quick JFK arrival checklist

  1. Confirm terminal & construction pickup notices. Check your live terminal in case airline gates or Terminal 5/7 roadway pickup lanes shifted.
  2. Keep your accommodation address handy offline. Screenshot your Manhattan, Brooklyn or Queens hotel address — useful at customs and for drivers.
  3. Sort out US data or eSIM before boarding. Download offline New York maps and activate your eSIM plan before leaving home Wi-Fi.
  4. Match rail vs. road to your arrival time. For weekday 15:00–20:00 arrivals into Midtown, AirTrain + LIRR via Jamaica is consistently faster than highway traffic.

Common questions

Is the JFK yellow taxi flat fare really $70 total to Manhattan?

The metered base flat fare between JFK and anywhere in Manhattan is $70, but NYC surcharges, MTA/congestion fees, airport access charges, expressway/tunnel tolls, the weekday 16:00–20:00 peak $5 surcharge and a 15–20% tip are added on top — expect around $85–$105+ out of pocket.

Does AirTrain JFK run 24 hours a day?

Yes, AirTrain JFK operates 24/7 across all airport terminals and both Jamaica and Howard Beach stations. Remember that late-night LIRR and Subway frequencies are lower than daytime schedules.

Should I go to Jamaica or Howard Beach from JFK?

Go to Jamaica Station for LIRR commuter trains to Penn Station or Grand Central Madison (~20 min) and the E, J or Z subway lines. Go to Howard Beach for the A subway line serving lower Manhattan and western Brooklyn.

Can I use my contactless bank card on AirTrain and the Subway?

Yes — both the AirTrain exit gates (at Jamaica/Howard Beach) and NYC Subway turnstiles accept contactless Visa, Mastercard, American Express and phone wallets via OMNY.
